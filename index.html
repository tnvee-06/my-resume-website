A Seat at the Table

Shikara International · Class X

Sapphire House Leader

The title sounded ceremonial. The work was anything but.

In Class X, I was trusted to lead Sapphire House at Shikara International. It meant representing the house, coordinating between students and teachers, and taking responsibility for something deceptively simple: getting people to show up, and to care when they did.

Much of the work happened behind the scenes. I helped organise school events, managed competitions, and kept participation steady across a house of students with very different strengths. It showed me that leadership is less about standing at the front and more about making sure everyone else can step forward.

That year

Best House Award

Awarded to Sapphire House during my year as House Leader: a result that belonged to every student in it.

“Leadership is making sure everyone else can step forward.”

Committee Rooms & Business Blueprints

conferences

The committee room · Model United Nations

Speaking for a country that wasn't mine, and meaning it.

Across four Model United Nations conferences, I learned what it takes to hold a position in a room full of people who disagree with it.

  1. I.

    Research

    Preparation involved examining each country's political context, international relationships, policy priorities and position on the agenda. This research provided the foundation for informed contributions during committee sessions.

  2. II.

    Representation

    Each conference required me to articulate the position of an assigned country accurately and consistently, including on issues where its stance differed from my personal perspective.

  3. III.

    Debate

    Committee proceedings involved presenting arguments, responding to opposing positions and contributing to structured discussions. These experiences strengthened my research, public speaking and ability to engage with differing viewpoints.

The lasting lesson was perspective: most issues look different depending on where you stand.

Blueprint · YEB-2025

Young Entrepreneurs Bootcamp

BITS Pilani, Hyderabad Campus · 2025

If the committee room was about words, the bootcamp was about making.

The Young Entrepreneurs Bootcamp at BITS Pilani provided exposure to the practical dimensions of entrepreneurship, with a focus on emerging technologies, product development and the application of digital tools.

At the Rakesh Kapoor Innovation Center (RKIC), I was introduced to prototyping infrastructure and technologies, including CNC machines, 3D printers and robotics sandboxes. The sessions offered insight into the processes and facilities involved in hardware experimentation and product development.

The programme also covered AI prompt engineering, including its potential applications in translating concepts into code and developing investor-oriented presentations.

The experience provided a broader perspective on how technological innovation, product development and business communication intersect in the entrepreneurial ecosystem.

The Details Behind Desire

Why does a piece of jewellery become a conversation? Why can an unexpected shape become iconic? And how does a fashion house turn imagination into an identity people recognise?

Cover story · Schiaparelli

Where jewellery stops being an accessory and becomes an idea.

Schiaparelli is the house I keep returning to. Its surrealist jewellery treats a ring or a brooch less like an ornament and more like a provocation: symbolism, art and imagination, given a shape and finished with extraordinary craftsmanship.

What fascinates me isn't only how striking the pieces are, but how consistently they say the same thing. I want to understand how a house protects something so imaginative, presents it, and keeps it desirable over time.

“A surreal detail becomes a signature. A signature becomes an identity.”
  1. iWhy do some objects become iconic?
  2. iiHow do heritage and innovation coexist?
  3. iiiHow does craftsmanship shape perceived value?
  4. ivHow do premium brands stay desirable over time?

My curiosity leans West, towards the great European houses. But it keeps finding its way home.

The European houses

Where I look to understand how heritage becomes strategy, and how desirability is kept alive across generations.

The Indian ateliers

Where Indian craft holds its own as luxury, carrying the memory of the hands that made it.

The Long View

I want to understand what makes a luxury brand endure.

Luxury endures for reasons that are rarely visible: a house's discipline about what it will not do, the patience of its craftsmanship, the consistency of the story it tells. I want to understand those decisions from the inside, across business, retail, branding and the psychology of the people who choose them.

I'm early in that pursuit, and deliberately so. The aim isn't a title. It's fluency: knowing why a house grows, how an identity holds, and what keeps a name meaningful long after it was first desired.
